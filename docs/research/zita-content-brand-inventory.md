### Recommended Artwork Content Standard

The information requirement, not the Shopify implementation.

#### Original Artwork

**Required**
- Title, and its meaning or translation where the title carries one
- Series
- Year completed
- Medium and substrate (for example: acrylic and mixed media on gallery-wrapped canvas)
- Dimensions in **both** inches and centimetres, plus depth
- Orientation (portrait, landscape, square)
- Availability, and whether sold work stays visible
- Price
- **Artwork story**, 60 to 150 words in her own voice
- Certificate of authenticity: whether one is included
- Ready to hang: yes or no, and how it is finished at the edges
- Shipping: where from, how long, how packed
- **At least 3 images:** clean artwork, artwork on a wall, one detail
- Alt text on every image

**Valuable where available**
- Exhibition history for that specific work
- Award or recognition
- Palette in her own words ("dark blues, copper and greys")
- Scale reference image

#### Prints

**Required**
- Which original it reproduces, and a link to it
- Printing method and paper or substrate
- Available sizes with dimensions in both units
- Border or bleed
- Edition: open or limited, and the number if limited
- Whether signed
- Framing: included, optional or not offered
- Production and shipping time
- **At least 2 images:** the print, and the print on a wall

## 4. Artwork storytelling

### 4.1 The voice Ranjeeta already uses

This is the most valuable finding in the audit. She has a real and consistent voice, and it should not be rewritten.

**Its characteristics, evidenced:**

1. **First person, plain, unguarded.** "At times, I get drawn towards simplicity and profoundness of Black and White."
2. **Philosophical without being pretentious.** From Prithvi - Earth: *"The significance of knowing that body is made of five elements in nature and will perish into these has helped me look inward and explore my existence beyond this body."*
3. **Sanskrit terminology, always translated, never explained at length.** "Prithvi in Sanskrit is Earth". "Jal in Sanskrit is water". Cultural specificity carried lightly.
4. **She names her own colours.** From Prithvi - Earth: *"Dark blues, copper and greys mark the variation and movement of the wind."* This is gold for brand work, because it is her language for her own palette.
5. **Observational and scene-based when she relaxes.** From Moonlit Waves: *"I deviated from the blue waters and orange skies to observe the sea at night. It was dark all around, the world has shut down, yet the ocean was soaring high and the moon illuminated the waves putting up a graceful show of light and sound."* That is the best sentence on the website.
6. **Ethical and outward-turning.** From the print: *"Like we admire a pretty garden filled with flowers of many colors, we need to open our heart to admire all the beautiful people on this planet."*
7. **Duality as a recurring idea.** *"It reminds me that light shines due to darkness, else we wouldn't have known light."*

**Recurring themes, all evidence-based:**

| Theme | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The five elements and impermanence of the body | Entire Elements series and its collection description |
| Looking inward, existence beyond the physical | Elements descriptions, About page |
| Nature as the source | About page: skies, oceans, mountains, air, spring |
| Light and dark as a pair | Yin and Yang works, Un Colored by Nature, Moonlit Waves |
| Flow, unpredictability, movement | About page: "the unpredictability and movement of colors" |
| Gratitude, and finding strength in pain | About page, closing paragraph |
| Openness to other people and perspectives | Print description, About page |

### Photography Homework for Ranjeeta

Realistic for one artist working alone. A single afternoon covers most of the MUST HAVE list.

**MUST HAVE**
1. **One room or wall photograph per available work.** This is the single highest-value asset in the whole audit, because it is the hover behaviour she specifically asked for and it is what makes artwork feel ownable. A consistent wall, consistent daylight, a sofa or console for scale. One session, one wall, every painting in turn.
2. **One clean, straight-on, colour-accurate image per work**, replacing any phone snapshot taken at an angle. Flat light, no glare, square to the canvas.
3. **Confirm which works already have room shots** so we do not reshoot what exists.

**HIGH VALUE**
4. **One detail or texture shot per work**, close enough to show the fluid pour and any texture elements. For a fluid artist this is the proof of technique, and it takes seconds per painting.
5. **Edge or profile shot** for gallery-wrapped works, showing how the sides are finished. One representative shot can stand for all works finished the same way.
6. **Four to six studio and process photographs.** Paint pouring, the work in progress, her hands, the studio wall. These carry the About page and the News section.
7. **Two or three new portraits**, including one working rather than posed.

**OPTIONAL**
8. Scale reference with a person in frame, for the large works.
9. Framed examples, only if she offers framing.
10. Short vertical process video clips, which double as social content.

**Assessment:** the raw material for three to four chapters already exists. What is missing is **scene**. There are no dates, no places, no named people, no described moments. Rinske Douna's story works because it contains a museum in Madrid, a named artist in Antwerp, a specific challenge with a number attached. Ranjeeta's contains the same kind of turning points stated as summary rather than shown.

**Verdict: there is enough for a chaptered About page, but not yet enough for an immersive one.** Six specific answers would close the gap.

### About Page Homework for Ranjeeta

Six questions. Two to four paragraphs each. Aim for scenes, not summaries.

1. **The glass years.** "You spent about six years working with fused glass. What drew you to it, and what did glass teach you that you still use in fluid painting? Was there a specific piece or moment when you realised you had outgrown it?"
2. **Coming back to painting.** "You came back to painting after your son was born. Can you describe the first time you painted again? Where were you in the house, what did you paint, what was the baby doing, and how did it feel to hold a brush after that gap?"
3. **The first pour.** "Can you remember the first time you poured paint and watched it move on its own? What surprised you? What did you do differently the next day?"
4. **The first sale.** "Your first commission came after your first Facebook share. Who was it, what did they ask for, and what did you feel when someone first paid for your work?"
5. **The Elements series.** "Elements was your first series in this style. What was happening in your life when you started it? The writing suggests you were thinking about the body and impermanence, and it would be good to say why, in as much or as little detail as you want to share."
6. **What you want a collector to feel.** "When someone hangs your painting in their home and looks at it on an ordinary Tuesday morning, what do you hope happens to them?"

**Two more, optional but valuable:** who or what influenced her, named specifically; and what she found hard, since the current story has no obstacle in it.
