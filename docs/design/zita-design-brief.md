# Zita's Art Studio — Design Brief

**Project:** Zita's Art Studio Shopify Redesign  
**Status:** Draft v1 — ready for review  
**Date:** 19 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Translate research, Ranjeeta's stated preferences, and the current content inventory into a clear visual and experience direction before prototyping or Shopify implementation.

> This document is the first prescriptive design artifact in the project.
>
> Research documents describe what exists.  
> `ranjeeta-preferences.md` records what Ranjeeta has explicitly said she wants.  
> This brief defines what the redesigned Zita's Art Studio should become.

---

## 1. Design North Star

Zita's Art Studio should feel like a **distinctive digital gallery for Ranjeeta's work**, not a conventional Shopify storefront.

The site should be:

- art-first
- visually sophisticated
- emotionally engaging
- editorial rather than template-driven
- calm enough to let the artwork breathe
- rich enough to feel intentional and memorable
- easy to browse
- easy to purchase from
- manageable by Ranjeeta without needing a developer for normal content changes

The primary emotional objective is:

# **Inspired**

A visitor should feel inspired before they feel "sold to."

Commerce remains essential, but it should sit naturally inside the gallery experience rather than visually dominating it.

### Core principle

**Art-first, commerce-enabled.**

### Approval question

> **Does this feel like Zita's Art Studio?**

### North Star

> **Would a visitor ever guess this was based on a standard Shopify theme?**

Ideally, no.

## 2. What the Site Should Make a Visitor Feel, Understand, Explore, and Do

Before implementing any major page, use this framework.

### Feel

The visitor should primarily feel:

- inspired
- curious
- reflective
- welcomed into Ranjeeta's artistic world
- confident that the work is presented professionally

Secondary emotional qualities may include:

- calm
- sophisticated
- intimate
- bold
- transported
- peaceful

The site does not need to express all of these at equal intensity on every page.

### Understand

A visitor should quickly understand:

- this is the work of a real practicing artist
- Ranjeeta creates in meaningful series
- nature, movement, duality, reflection, and fluidity are central to her practice
- original work and more accessible print options may coexist
- Ranjeeta has an exhibition history and artistic recognition
- commissions are available
- this is a personal artist studio, not an anonymous ecommerce brand

### Explore

Visitors should be encouraged to explore:

- active series
- individual artworks
- sold work as part of Ranjeeta's artistic archive
- Ranjeeta's story and process
- exhibitions and recognition
- prints where available
- commissions
- News / Press
- Instagram

### Do

Valid outcomes include:

- purchase an original
- purchase a print
- enquire about a commission
- join the email list
- follow Ranjeeta on Instagram
- explore exhibitions
- return for future work

The redesign should **not** force all visitors through a single transactional funnel.

The two primary reference sites remain useful, but they serve different roles.

Ranjeeta prefers the overall **feeling** of Dimitra Milan.

Useful qualities to adopt or adapt:

- richer gallery atmosphere
- sophisticated typography
- editorial rhythm
- artistic About-page presentation
- large artwork presence
- confident use of color

- prominent newsletter opportunities

Useful qualities to adopt or adapt:

- quiet interface chrome
- clean visual restraint

Zita needs its own answers for:

- collection pages that feel curated rather than generic
- a high-value original-artwork detail experience
- sold-work archive behavior
- original ↔ print relationships
- exhibition storytelling
- homepage rhythm
- teal/nature-led visual identity
- motion language
- image-ratio consistency
- integration of Instagram, newsletter, exhibitions, and commissions without clutter

## 5. Visual Direction

### Confirmed direction

Ranjeeta wants:

- a richer, more artistic experience than her current site
- less continuous plain white
- a more layered page atmosphere
- Dimitra-like visual richness
- a palette that comes from her own work rather than from the reference sites
- teal as a strong candidate because it aligns with her nature-inspired work
- openness to darker sections if they look good
- artwork to remain visually dominant

### Brand-reference artwork

Ranjeeta selected **Vaayu - Air** as the artwork that best represents how she wants the website to feel.

Current storefront reference:

`https://www.zitasartstudio.com/products/perfect-storm?variant=37178945798331`

This artwork should be treated as a primary visual reference during brand exploration, alongside the broader representative artwork set already identified in the content inventory.

### Palette direction

The palette should be **artwork-derived**, not selected from generic brand-color trends.

Strong candidate roles:

- deep teal / blue-green anchor
- oceanic blue or mineral blue
- pale blue-grey / mist tone
- warm sand / stone neutral
- restrained copper / antique gold counterpoint
- warm off-white or light natural canvas tone
- occasional artwork-derived accent color

No hex values are approved yet.

### Design requirement

Claude Design should sample and compare colors from the actual artwork rather than choosing an arbitrary teal.

### Dark-mode usage

A dark website is **not** a requirement.

Dark teal, ink, indigo, or near-black sections may be explored where they:

- enhance artwork
- improve page rhythm
- create contrast between editorial moments
- remain accessible
- do not overpower the paintings

A mostly light site with occasional dark tonal passages may prove stronger than an entirely dark treatment.

## 8. Artwork Photography & Media System

This is a core part of the brand experience, not a merchandising afterthought.

### Confirmed

Ranjeeta already has room/interior images for many products, especially within **Walking the Trail**.

The current problem is **inconsistent image aspect ratios and presentation**.

### Preferred product-card behavior

Desktop:

1. primary state — clean artwork
2. hover state — artwork shown in a room/interior
3. click — full artwork-detail experience

Mobile:

Hover does not exist, so the secondary image must remain discoverable through:

- swipe
- secondary preview
- PDP gallery
- or another touch-appropriate interaction

### Important design principle

**Do not destructively crop the artwork merely to make a grid look consistent.**

Instead, explore a consistent presentation field or image canvas where the complete artwork can be shown using contain-style behavior where necessary.

The frame around the artwork may be visually consistent even when the artwork's own aspect ratio is not.

### Product media hierarchy

Where available:

1. clean full artwork
2. room/interior image
3. texture/detail
4. edge/profile
5. scale reference
6. framing or installation
7. exhibition image where relevant

### Future photography improvements

High-value additions include:

- consistent room photography
- detail / texture photographs
- studio/process imagery
- new artist portraits
- exhibition installation photography

Existing Shopify imagery is sufficient for initial brand and prototype work. High-resolution source files are useful but should not block design.

## 9. Global Page Rhythm

The site should not feel like:

> section → section → section → section

with every block using identical spacing, width, and alignment.

Instead, establish a flexible editorial rhythm through:

- changes in content width
- intentional full-bleed artwork moments
- narrow editorial text passages
- generous negative space
- tonal background transitions
- occasional asymmetry
- varied image scale
- selective overlaps where useful
- strong typographic pauses
- restrained motion

Different page types may use different compositions while sharing the same visual system.

## 11. Homepage Experience

The homepage should introduce Ranjeeta's artistic world, not merely display products.

### Homepage objective

A first-time visitor should quickly:

1. encounter strong artwork
2. feel inspired
3. understand that the work belongs to a real artist with a point of view
4. discover the active series
5. see a path to originals and prints
6. become curious about Ranjeeta
7. encounter selective credibility
8. have opportunities to follow or stay connected

### Suggested narrative structure

This is a design framework, not a fixed section order.

#### 1. Opening artwork moment

Avoid a generic ecommerce hero banner.

Explore:

- a large artwork-led composition
- possible asymmetry
- minimal copy
- strong visual relationship to Vaayu - Air / the emerging palette
- subtle motion only if it helps

The opening should feel like entering a gallery.

#### 2. Active series discovery

Present the current 2–3 active series prominently.

This should feel curated, not like a standard collection-card grid.

Possible approaches:

- varied image scale
- horizontal editorial rhythm
- one large series + smaller supporting series
- staggered compositions
- text integrated into artwork fields

#### 3. Selected artworks

Show a small, intentional edit of work rather than a large generic product grid.

Potential mix:

- available original
- sold work
- room-view image
- a work with a strong story

#### 4. Artist point of view

Use a short excerpt from Ranjeeta's existing writing to introduce the ideas behind her practice.

Link into About.

#### 5. Recognition / exhibition moment

Use one selective proof point rather than a wall of credentials.

Possible ingredients:

- Curator's Choice
- juried international selection
- current/upcoming exhibition

Keep it elegant and contextual.

#### 6. Originals / prints pathway

Provide a clear route for visitors who love the work but cannot purchase an original.

Prints should feel like an accessible extension of the art, not a discount aisle.

#### 7. Commission invitation

A concise, personal invitation.

#### 8. Stay connected

Newsletter and Instagram should both be available without competing with the artwork.

### Homepage should not become

- a giant product catalogue
- a carousel-heavy theme demo
- a stack of generic Shopify cards
- a collection of promotional banners
- a wall of sales CTAs

## 13. Product Listing Page (PLP)

This decision is now substantially resolved.

### Ranjeeta's explicit preference

Use **Rinske Douna's artwork-first PLP philosophy** as the stronger reference.

### Desired desktop behavior

- artwork uses most of the available width
- approximately **3 columns** as a baseline
- Filter & Sort available on demand
- no permanent left filter sidebar by default
- generous spacing
- restrained product metadata
- room-image hover where available
- full artwork remains visible

### Why this suits Zita

Current design interpretation:

- the catalogue is relatively small
- only 2–3 series are expected to be foregrounded
- the site functions partly as an artistic portfolio/archive
- visual impact matters more than maximizing product density
- persistent facets would consume valuable gallery space

This interpretation should be validated through prototype review rather than treated as an immutable law.

### Responsive behavior

Likely:

- large desktop — 3 columns
- smaller desktop/tablet — 2 columns
- mobile — 1 or 2 depending on legibility and image treatment

Do not force three columns everywhere.

### Filters

Potential useful filters may eventually include:

- availability
- medium
- size
- orientation
- series
- price

Only expose filters that are supported by clean product data and genuinely help discovery.

### Design opportunity

Explore a **Zita-specific variation** where a regular grid can occasionally be interrupted by:

- a larger featured artwork
- a series statement
- an editorial image
- an exhibition reference

This should never make browsing confusing.

## 15. Original Artwork PDP

The original-artwork page should feel like an **artwork detail / gallery page with purchasing available**, not a standard product detail page.

### Primary objectives

A visitor should:

- see the artwork at meaningful scale
- understand what it is
- understand why it exists
- understand physical size and material
- imagine it in a space
- know whether it is available
- know how purchasing/shipping works
- feel confident enough to buy or enquire

### Information hierarchy

Likely order:

1. artwork title
2. large artwork imagery
3. price / availability
4. artwork story
5. series
6. dimensions
7. year
8. medium / substrate
9. room image / scale context
10. detail / texture
11. ready-to-hang / framing information
12. certificate information
13. shipping information
14. exhibition history where relevant
15. purchase or enquiry action
16. related works / series continuation
17. print relationship if one exists

### Sold originals

Sold works remain visible.

A sold work should:

- clearly say Sold
- remove purchase confusion
- preserve the story and images
- remain discoverable within the series/archive
- optionally point to related available work or prints

### Do not

- use quantity steppers for unique originals
- use fake urgency
- use countdowns
- use star ratings
- bury the artwork story under ecommerce controls

## 24. Mobile Experience

Mobile is not a reduced desktop design.

### Requirements

- artwork remains visually dominant
- navigation remains clear
- series discovery works without hover
- room-image behavior has a touch equivalent
- filters open as an accessible drawer/sheet
- typography remains comfortable
- touch targets are generous
- image loading is efficient
- editorial compositions simplify gracefully
- no essential meaning depends on animation

### Avoid

- desktop compositions simply squeezed into a narrow column
- tiny product metadata
- overly tall stacked utility bars
- hidden purchasing actions
- interaction patterns requiring hover

## 25. Motion & Interaction

Motion should feel subtle, slow enough to read, and subordinate to artwork.

Potential uses:

- gentle artwork reveal
- restrained image crossfade
- room-view hover
- slow text/image entrance
- subtle header behavior
- editorial scroll transitions

### Avoid

- excessive parallax
- constant motion
- autoplay effects that compete with paintings
- cursor gimmicks
- dramatic transitions that slow shopping
- animation purely because a tool can generate it

Support reduced-motion preferences.

- keyboard navigation

- sufficient contrast
- meaningful image alt text
- correct button/link semantics
- touch-friendly controls
- readable body typography
- reduced-motion support
- accessible filter drawer
- accessible menu behavior
- no text embedded into imagery where it needs to be read
- product state conveyed by more than color alone

## 27. Performance

The artwork is image-heavy, so performance must be designed intentionally.

### Principles

- responsive Shopify image transforms
- appropriate image sizing
- lazy loading below the fold
- avoid unnecessarily huge image files
- minimal JavaScript
- CSS/native browser capabilities first
- restrained animation
- no unnecessary frontend framework
- audit third-party scripts
- avoid app bloat
- preserve perceived speed on mobile

High-resolution source artwork should not simply be delivered directly to every visitor.

## 30. What the Design Should NOT Become

This section is intentionally explicit.

Do not design Zita as:

### A standard Shopify storefront

Avoid:

- repeated section stacks
- uniform card grids everywhere
- generic hero banners
- generic "featured collection" sections
- ecommerce badges dominating the art
- predictable theme-demo layouts

### A Dimitra Milan clone

Do not:

- copy Dimitra's page compositions wholesale
- copy the blush/red palette
- reproduce distinctive text or graphics
- mirror entire page layouts
- substitute Ranjeeta's art into Dimitra's structure without reinterpretation

### A Rinske Douna clone

Do not:

- copy the exact product grid
- copy Dawn-like page rhythm
- adopt green simply because Rinske uses it
- inherit utility choices that do not serve Zita

### An over-designed art-school experiment

Avoid:

- navigation that sacrifices clarity
- excessive asymmetry
- motion for its own sake
- text that becomes hard to read
- interactions that confuse purchasing
- visual novelty that competes with artwork

### A luxury cliché

Avoid relying on:

- generic beige minimalism
- excessive all-caps serif typography
- empty aspirational phrases
- artificial scarcity
- "exclusive luxury" language disconnected from Ranjeeta's voice

## 32. Success Criteria for the Prototype

The first prototype succeeds if:

- it feels specific to Ranjeeta
- teal/nature influence feels derived from the art, not generic
- typography feels sophisticated and readable
- Dimitra's atmosphere is felt without copying Dimitra

- artwork dominates

- navigation is immediately understandable
- series are easy to discover
- PLP feels gallery-like
- filters remain available but secondary
- buying remains clear

### Story

- visitors understand there is a real artist behind the work
- exhibitions/recognition add credibility without overwhelming the art
